# Negotiation AI study · 20 October 2026

The current AI is **budgeted sampled expectimax, not a proven optimal solver**. This report measures the unchanged playable implementation. No balance rules, spell effects or search filters were retuned for the study.

## Algorithm

The player maximizes expected utility over Confront, Deflect, withdrawal and a filtered set of spells. There is no opponent minimization node: the opponent presents stochastic arguments rather than strategically choosing a response. Consequently this is expectimax, not the combat prototype’s alpha–beta minimax. At each chance node, success/rejection or deflection Suspicion is weighted by its actual probability. Unknown future arguments use three deterministic stratified samples of the public weighted pool; sibling spell alternatives share those samples. Foresight uses the actual revealed next argument. The policy never receives hidden card RNG, check RNG or the unrevealed pending argument.

Iterative deepening attempts one, two and three response turns, retaining the last completed depth. A spell does not consume a response depth, allowing cast-then-respond plans. The cooperative 50 ms budget reserves 3 ms, with 18,000 budget checks and a per-decision transposition cache. If no depth completes, it uses a one-step non-spell fallback.

Terminal utility is 1 for full agreement, 0.1 for partial agreement and 0 for failure, minus small Mana/crown/Health expenses (0.00004/0.00004/0.00008 per unit). The leaf estimates a race between required successful leads and remaining rejection capacity, adjusting for average Agreement reward, Patience/Suspicion costs, current Resolve and current check chance. It blends current-card and pool averages 55:45. **Mood and Alignment loss are not priced in this utility.** Partial agreements and reduced resource spending can therefore trade against the full-win rate.

Optimality is limited by three-card sampling, a three-turn horizon, the heuristic leaf, candidate pruning and time limits. Some spells are excluded before search; a zero usage rate for those spells is not evidence that exhaustive search found them weak.

## Study configuration

1536 games: 1440 gap-comparison games and 96 spell-access diagnostics. Each cell has 32 paired seeds. Base seed 20261020; episode seeds use the saved manifest’s unsigned integer formula. The same episode seeds are reused across policies, levels and gaps. Sources, hashes, Node version, configurations, per-move decisions, eligibility counts, action tapes and settlements are frozen in the results directory. All 1536 tapes replayed exactly. Timed AI choices can vary between machines or reruns even when a saved command tape remains exact.

Main matrix: Balanced kit, levels 20/50/80, Mood 70, full Mana, neutral Alignment, and level-matched Renown/relationship/preparation/performance. Opponent level means **check DC**, not a symmetric NPC skill kit. Gap = opponent DC − player level. Pool is the full 45-card calibration pool, with no crown/Health requests, flag gates or underground permission. These are not all-encounter win rates.

Each opponent DC also sets meeting limits: band = floor((DC−1)/20), Agreement = 7+band, Suspicion = 14+band, Patience = Suspicion+8+floor(DC/25). This measures the complete configured encounter difficulty, including longer requirements at higher DC, rather than isolating only the check-probability effect. At equal DC 20/50/80 those limits are 7/22/14, 9/26/16 and 10/28/17 (Agreement/Patience/Suspicion).

## Identified evaluation flaw

The nonterminal leaf can become negative because a Suspicion penalty is subtracted from a small estimated win probability. Immediate withdrawal has failure utility zero (less already-spent resource costs), so search can prefer surrender despite a positive chance of eventual agreement. In this no-payment pool, Confront spends no additional Mana/crowns/Health, and Mood is unpriced. Where a positive-outcome path remains, continued confrontation dominates immediate withdrawal under the stated terminal objective. No correction was applied during this measurement.

An inspected trace: level 20 versus DC 30, episode 3, turn 8. The player had 0/8 Agreement, 15 Patience, 10/15 Suspicion, zero Resolve, and a preparation lead with 34% acceptance. The leaf was approximately −0.00691; immediate withdrawal scored 0 and was selected. This is a concrete reason the results must not be called optimal. Shadowstep partial banking is different: it can rationally trade a risky full win for guaranteed partial utility.

## Interpretation

The measured difficulty curve is steep: same-level full wins are roughly one half, while +10 DC falls to 6–13% and +20 to 0–3% in this sample. This includes DC-dependent meeting lengths and does not establish a true optimal-player ceiling. Search improves on the shallow approximation in pooled full wins, but does not outperform always confronting in the full grid. Partial utility and spell expenses do not establish a clear overall advantage either.

Ordinary-kit spell variety is narrow: three spells are used, with Glamour accounting for about 84% of casts. With unrestricted spell access, Candour/Quickquill/Rapport account for about 97% of casts and wins rise to 29/32 at level 50/DC 50. Showman favors Rapport/Reflect instead. This shows distinct kit strategies, but concentrated choices within each kit. These are diagnostic observations, not controlled estimates of each spell’s independent contribution.

Before treating this AI as an optimal balance benchmark, the evaluator needs to avoid unjustified surrender, the intended value of Mood/Alignment and partial agreements needs to be specified, and restrictive spell filters need review. The present study records the current behavior without applying those fixes.
