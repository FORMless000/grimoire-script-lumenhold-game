## Lore

- **the Tide Caves** (`tide_caves`, soft): Under the headland past Port Calder's breakwater, sea caves open at low water and close on the flood. Smugglers have run goods through their dry upper galleries for generations, under the customs men's feet; a brick culvert climbs from them to the bonded warehouses. At the far end a great arch, the sea's door, opens on the open sea, with a ship carved over it by nobody knows whom.

## Place: The Old Hall (`old_hall`)

countryside · open afternoon, evening, night

The valley's shut manor behind its wall at the moor's edge, empty since the family went away in a bad year; a land agent from the capital has bought it, and a lamp burns in the study at night.

Teaser while undiscovered: At the Drovers' Inn, they say somebody from the capital has bought the old hall.

## Lore

- **the old hall** (`old_hall`, soft): The valley's shut manor behind its wall at the moor's edge. The family who built it kept the valley's books, the ones that matter, until they sold up and left in a bad year nobody will date; an old caretaker stayed. This year a land agent from the capital bought it and works at the old family's desk.
- **wall stair** (`tower_wall_stair`, soft): Inside the seventh tower's wall, behind a rack of empty bottles in the cellar, a servants' stair one shoulder wide climbs to the landing of candles, with a spyhole onto the main stair. Above the landing are the old classrooms, shut with the tower a century ago and left as their teachers left them: an examiner's cupboard of old papers, floorboards that creak each a different note, a brass orrery still turning, a professor's portrait that asks what you think you're doing. The class register ends on its last day: "Tower closed. Lessons moved." Two turns below the top, where desks choke the stair, the duelling master's study stays locked, and nobody has had its key since.
- **Sorting Mirror** (`sorting_mirror`, soft): A tall enchanted mirror in the entrance hall. It does not sort anyone into anything; it asks, in a voice like cold water, who you were before you came.
- **Pell & Daughters** (`pell_and_daughters`, soft): The village apothecary, with a thousand labelled drawers. Old Pell taught three generations to count drops; his daughters keep the counter now, and he counts aloud behind them. It buys what foragers bring out of the Whisperwood.
- **the Tenant** (`the_tenant`, soft): The Tenant keeps a long hall under the hollow hill on the moor, where a harvest supper is always laid. It is tall and wheat-crowned, with a face like a field seen from a moving cart, courteous and hungry for company rather than flesh: its guests eat, laugh and do not age at its table for as long as the bargain holds. It answers the door in the hill only to someone who knows what is behind it.
