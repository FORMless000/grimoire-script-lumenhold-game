# Local context packet: `content/events/between.grim`

Evidence only for the 15 scoped cards. This packet records source facts and mechanics; it makes no critic verdict and edits no source content.

## Delivery and cooldown context

- All 15 events are in `between.grim` lines 11–24: pools `between` and `between_once`, both `trigger: social`, class `routine`, weight 1. `between` sets `repeat: 8`; `between_once` uses per-card `seen(<id>) == 0` gates (the pool comment explains this) [source: `content/events/between.grim:11-24`].
- The 15 events are: `between_two_seats`, `between_messenger`, `between_whose_idea`, `between_boot_hex`, `between_not_them`, `between_peace_offering`, `between_seconds`, `between_dorm_vote`, `between_lab_partners`, `between_cipher`, `between_inside_joke`, `between_cover_roll`, `between_closed_table`, `between_night_roof`, and `between_falling_out` [source: `content/events/between.grim:28,56,83,107,130,153,181,212,234,264,288,309,340,365,388`].
- For these cards, `time:` is a preferred-time weighting, not a hard slot. Explicit declarations are `noon evening` for `between_two_seats` [line 36], `evening night` for `between_dorm_vote` [line 220], `morning noon afternoon` for `between_cover_roll` [line 317], and `noon evening` for `between_closed_table` [line 348]. `between_night_roof` alone declares `slot: night` [line 373]. Other cards have neither field.
- Design §4.2 says the term day has a noon social slot and `spread: social world` distributes social/world cards across day slots; when an event declares `time: evening`, it is drawn three times as often then and a third as often elsewhere [source: `docs/design-v0.3.md:313-326`, especially 317-326; game declaration at 331-338]. Therefore the plain `trigger: social` pool does not mean every event is noon-only. The night-roof card's explicit `slot: night` is materially different from a `time: night` preference.

## Cast conditions and what they establish

- `at_odds(a,b)` means `npc_rel(a,b) <= -5`; `get_along(a,b)` means `npc_rel(a,b) >= 5` [source: `content/core/conds.grim:43-52`].
- `classmate_here(x)` expands to `tagged(x, peer) and not tagged(x, outsider) and met(x) and around(x)` [source: `content/core/conds.grim:54-57`]. Engine evaluation treats `tagged` as an explicit tag or matching role, and `around` as a defined character who is not away [source: `src/engine.js:1404,1408`; `src/grimoire.js:27` documents around as reachable in person]. This enforces peer/not-outsider classification, prior meeting, and current availability; it does not test course enrollment/attendance, house, or dorm membership.
- The exception worth tracking is `between_cover_roll.b`: it repeats the peer/not-outsider/met checks directly but does **not** require `around(b)` [source: `content/events/between.grim:313-318`]. This fits an absent-seat premise at the level of cast eligibility; the cast text itself does not establish why that particular absent peer is away.
- `between_lab_partners` has a separate `when: term and not wrapup and enrolled(alchemy)` gate [source: `content/events/between.grim:234-242`]. This checks the player's enrollment in alchemy. It does not establish that either cast peer is enrolled in or scheduled for alchemy, or that the event is dealt in the course appointment. Course appointment is described as one card per course enrolled, with its professor and whoever is there, at the `courses` slot [source: `content/core/appointments.grim:39-44`]. The event itself is in a `trigger: social` pool [between.grim:11-24].
- The conditions do not enforce shared house/dorm. In particular, `between_dorm_vote` uses `classmate_here` for both cast roles but no house condition [between.grim:212-220], and `between_night_roof` likewise has no common-dorm condition [between.grim:365-373]. The descriptions place students in a common room/dormitory, but that placement is not encoded in the cast filters.
- `tagged(x, peer)` includes NPCs carrying the `peer` tag, while `tagged(x, outsider)` also matches role `outsider` [source: `src/engine.js:1408`]. For a concrete edge case, Ilse has `peer` among tags but role `outsider`, so she is excluded by `not tagged(..., outsider)` [source: `content/world/cast.grim:253-263`]. Tamsin's role is outsider and they are distant, so they also do not meet `classmate_here` [source: `content/world/cast.grim:273-293`; around semantics above].

## Named cast, voice, facts, and pronoun tokens

- The cards use generic cast slots `{a}` and `{b}` rather than fixed speakers. The resulting dialogue is still spoken by the chosen NPC and must match that NPC's voice and facts. For example, Lyra's voice is defined at `content/world/cast.grim:9-29`, Bram's at `164-185`, and Ilse's at `253-271`. Kael and Bram are explicitly excluded from selected cards with `not npc_is(kael)`/`not npc_is(bram)` at `between.grim:269-270,293-294,370-371`, but most cards permit any matching peer. Generic casting is not an exemption from canon or voice review.
- Pronoun expansion is documented in `docs/grimoire-script.md:345-354`: actor tokens `{they}`, `{them}`, `{their}`, `{theirs}` use the actor's pronouns; `{b.theirs}` / `{b.them}` use a specific character's pronoun. Verb agreement uses `{are}` and `{s}`. The engine's pronoun map is `src/engine.js:14-18` (she/her, he/him, they/them mappings).
- Visible event usages include `{them}`, `{their}`, `{they}`, `{b.theirs}`, `{b.their}`, `{b.them}`, and `{they} say{s}` in `between.grim:32-55,95-105,154-177,193-208,321-338`. These should be interpreted as templated cast-pronoun tokens, not fixed gender claims. Notably the docs warn against contractions like `{they}'s` and `{they}'ve` [grimoire-script.md:354]; `between_cover_roll` uses `{b}'s` as a contraction with the name token, not `{they}'s`.

## `between_cover_roll`: detention effect evidence

- Source says on a failed deception check: “It's a detention for you and the letter home for {b} anyway” [source: `content/events/between.grim:322-334`]. The actual effects under `lose:` are `rel a +tiny`, `rel b +tiny`, `mood -small`, and `alignment -tiny`; there is no `gain detention` effect [same lines 329-334]. The success effects likewise only change relationships/alignment [lines 323-328]. The “stay quiet” option changes `rel a` and alignment only [lines 335-338].
- The state implementation shows what active detention does: `@state detention`, `duration: 1`, with `day:` routing afternoon and evening to the detention pool; break clears it [source: `content/states/states.grim:401-412`]. Design §4.2 says this replaces both monthly visits with detention cards and misses those visit obligations [source: `docs/design-v0.3.md:344`]. The event's prose alone does not enact this state in the failure branch.
